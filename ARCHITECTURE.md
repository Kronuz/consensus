# Architecture

`consensus` is a deterministic Raft core and the pieces that run it: a persistence `Worker` over [journal](https://github.com/Kronuz/journal), snapshot transfer, and an optional authenticated peer transport. This document describes each layer and its contract with the host. Measurements and qualification records are in [docs/MEASUREMENTS.md](docs/MEASUREMENTS.md).

## The core: host contract

Construct the core with a `FixedConfiguration` (the founding membership), a semantically validated `RecoveredState`, resource limits, timing bounds, and a replication `Flow`. Founding voter IDs are nonzero, unique, and include the local node; membership changes after founding go through the log (below). Three or five voters are the intended authority deployment. Discovery and observed liveness never change the voting denominator. Provision unique cluster/configuration identities externally; bind them to every authenticated transport session before passing `Receive` events.

The host owns one protocol executor and supplies absolute monotonic `Tick` events, with election delays chosen externally within the configured range. Deliver current time before dispatching traffic or completions after slow I/O. While storage is pending, time continues to advance. A matching completion immediately releases its dependent effects and drives leader replication, but never initiates another campaign. Campaigns require a separately admitted Tick after storage becomes available; it may use the same absolute time as a Tick received while busy. Outdated ticks are ignored. Timing affects availability, not the voting or replication evidence required for commitment.

```text
Event → Core::step → Persist(token, batch) → ordered storage worker
                                 ↓ durable completion
                         Persisted(token) → Send / Committed
                                             ↓ application worker
                                       Applied(through)
```

Only one storage batch is outstanding. Stage changes internally, but never release dependent protocol replies, proposal placement, or application delivery before the matching completion. Unknown or duplicate completion tokens cannot release another batch. Storage failure fences the core. Local application completions remain reliable even while a later storage batch is pending; only retryable peer messages may be dropped. A transport receipt is not a Raft acknowledgement.

`ProposalPlaced` means local durable placement, not client write success. A client result comes from deterministic application of a committed command. The application supplies idempotency, transaction conflict evaluation, and bounded client-request ownership. Commands are opaque and may encode an admitted group of application transactions; grouping is necessary to amortize the journal's measured durability barriers.

The core persists term/vote changes before election traffic, appends a persisted current-term no-op on election, and advances commitment only from distinct configured-voter progress at a current-term log position. Commit-index advances are persisted before application delivery. Each peer has up to `Flow::pipeline` correlated Append RPCs in flight (one by default; see Replication); obsolete replies cannot manufacture or rewind progress, while a structurally valid higher term is observed before correlation filtering. A failure that cannot usefully decrease the next index backs off instead of forming an immediate request/response loop.

Only one bounded committed range awaits `Applied`. Completions must acknowledge that delivered range exactly. For reads, a leader first needs a committed current-term entry, then a new correlated quorum probe issued after the read's admission. Its applied cursor must reach the captured committed index before `ReadReady`. Probes preserve existing RPC flights and stop requesting contributions already counted or no longer needed for quorum. Leadership loss rejects pending reads. There are no leases or recent-traffic shortcuts.

## Membership

A group's membership lives in its log (`membership.h`): `Configuration` entries carry the members, each a per-group incarnation id (never reused) on a node identity, as a `Voter`, `Learner`, or `Witness`, with the next incarnation counter and, during a replacement, the voter `leaving`. A node uses the newest `Configuration` entry in its log, committed or not, and a replaced suffix rolls it back; `RecoveredState::membership` and the version 3 checkpoint bundle carry the membership in force at the base. A fixed configuration is the founding membership (every voter its own incarnation): with no change, its storage and checkpoint bytes are unchanged.

A leader admits `ChangeMembership{request, next}` only after it committed an entry of its own term and while no configuration is uncommitted (`can_change_membership()`), and only for one voter or witness added or removed with any learners; a learner is promoted once its matched index reaches the commit index (`progress()`). A promotion that names a `leaving` voter is a replacement: once it commits, whichever leader holds that membership removes the leaving voter, so the replacement completes across leader changes. Commit, elections, and reads count the voters and witnesses in force; a leader counts itself only while it is one, and steps down once its own removal commits. Learners replicate and never vote or campaign. The core admits messages from any incarnation the host authenticated, since a follower can lag the membership that made its sender leader; a ballot may name any incarnation. A joining node starts with an empty membership and learns it from the log.

## Elections: pre-vote, the leader-contact guard, and CheckQuorum

An expired election timer sends `PreVoteRequest` at the next term without changing the node's term or ballot; a voter grants it when the candidate's log is at least as up to date and it has not heard from a current leader within `election_min`, and answers without changing its own term. Only a quorum of grants starts the real campaign, so a node that cannot win (a stale log, a partition, a fast clock) never raises the group's term. A refusal that carries a higher term updates the pre-candidate's term. A voter that heard from its leader within `election_min` ignores a `VoteRequest` unless its `transfer` flag is set (leadership transfer). A leader steps down when fewer than a quorum of its voters, itself included, answered within `election_max` (CheckQuorum), so a leader that can send but not receive cannot hold the group. A single voter campaigns at once. These affect availability only; votes still require the log comparison and the durable ballot. Messages use the `DTC2` codec (pre-vote request and response, the vote request's transfer flag); `DTC1` frames are rejected.

`test/simulation_test.cc` checks the protocol over randomized groups (loss, reordering, duplication, partitions, skewed clocks, crashes that keep or lose a pending batch, repairs) against election safety, leader completeness, log matching, one applied prefix, one grant per voter per term, and durability when sent; `tools/mutants.sh` seeds known faults and requires the harness to catch each.

## Replication: pipelined appends

`Flow{pipeline, window_bytes}` sets how many Append RPCs and bytes a leader keeps in flight per peer. The default, one RPC, is stop-and-wait; a membership group's host raises it (the design uses 4). Each flight records its start and an optimistic `next`. A peer runs one flight until a first success verifies it, and drops back to one after a failure (resuming at that flight's start) or a timeout (resuming at the oldest flight's start). A success retires every older flight. Keepalives travel outside the window. `Flow::frame_bytes` (0 by default, leaving `rpc_bytes` in force) caps each append's payload, so a control frame on a shared connection waits behind at most one such frame; an entry above the cap goes alone. On the follower, appends that arrive while a batch persists are held (`WorkerLimits::follower_batch_bytes` and `follower_batch_entries`, 0 by default) and stepped as one `Receive` with `more`, so they reach storage as one batch while each is still answered with its own token.

## Repair at or below the commit cursor

`Repair{request, boundary}` restores a voter's application without touching consensus state: the host rebuilds its application image at `boundary` (from a pinned local checkpoint or a member's image, verified whole), and the core rewinds its applied cursor there and delivers the committed entries after it again from its own retained log. Hard state, role, leader, and commit index stay as they are, so the voter keeps voting. The boundary must lie in `[base_index, commit]` with its term in the log, so a host whose application stalled behind can restore a member's newer image; the core refuses a repair while busy or with a delivery outstanding (`Busy`), on a leader of more than one voter (`LeaderMustTransfer`: transfer leadership first), or at any other boundary (`InvalidRepair`). A repair advances the application epoch (`Core::epoch()`, `Worker::epoch()`); `LocalCheckpoint` and the Worker's `CaptureMetadata` carry the epoch the capture was taken under, and an older one is rejected, so a capture of damaged state never becomes the base, even one attached after the repair. Captures live in memory and do not survive a restart, so the epoch need not either. The Worker refuses a repair while a local checkpoint is in flight. A host that detects damage keeps acknowledging deliveries without applying them and repairs; it never reports the damage as `Failed{Application}`, which fences the core. Reads wait, as always, for the applied cursor to reach their captured index; the host serves no stale-tolerant read until `applied` returns to its position before the repair and does not report redelivered verdicts again.

## Durable adapter

`storage.h` encodes exact configuration initialization and opaque `StorageBatch` operations for the generic journal. Related hard-state and logical suffix changes fit in one atomic batch. Replacing an uncommitted Raft suffix appends a storage operation; it never physically truncates acknowledged journal history.

`storage_batch_size()` provides checked framing bounds without allocation or IO, and its batch overload measures the actual encoded size. Encoding uses the same helper before reserving output capacity. Admission can plan hard-state and log continuation batches without duplicating their format constants. These helpers do not reserve capacity or admit Core events; the worker supplies that enforcement.

`plan_event()` in `admission.h` returns a fixed pack of at most three append bounds. Tick campaigning reserves ballot, no-op and commit continuations; proposals reserve Normal command storage and a Control commit continuation. Any Command in an append RPC, including an empty payload, classifies the entire atomic batch as Normal. Internal persistence/application completions, faults and raw checkpoint events reject through this external-event planner. The worker owns their reliable delivery and checkpoint capture separately. Planning validates typed RPC payload/count bounds even while busy and uses exhaustive visitors to require a policy for future event variants. It performs no IO or Core mutation and is not itself enforcement.

## The persistence Worker

`Worker` in `worker.h` privately owns Core, Store, semantic recovery and scheduling state on one executor. Its IO backend is exclusive and outlives the worker and escaped recovery readers. `create()` establishes generic storage and queues typed initialization; `recover(restore_application)` verifies history and reconstructs unpublished state. `run_one(Tick)` advances bounded startup inventory and initialization before `ready()` allows protocol admission. A verified empty version 1 frontier can resume interrupted typed initialization; missing or damaged bootstrap metadata still fails closed. A restore callback builds unpublished application state, which becomes usable only after recovery succeeds.

`try_submit(Event)` acquires the complete continuation pack before stepping Core. Pressure releases every partial reservation without changing protocol state. There is no input queue. One persistence action, durable completion, application completion and complete external-action batch are retained. `take_actions()` drains the batch; occupied output blocks further protocol steps while pending IO and maintenance can still proceed. Matching `applied()` is reliable during busy persistence. `application_failed(index, reason)` fences the owned Core and Store for the outstanding delivered index; stale reports are ignored. A terminal notice remains deliverable after an older output batch drains. External persistence completions, application completions, faults and raw checkpoints reject through `try_submit()`.

`try_submit_retained(Event&)` shares the admission algorithm while preserving the caller's event and payload allocation on Busy or Pressure. Use it to retain one bounded inbound message while pausing further transport reads. Admitted execution moves the event into Core; discard it after Accepted or Fenced. The existing by-value API and durable/legacy wire formats are unchanged. Retained admission checks run in the Worker suite; `cluster_worker_test --retained-admission-benchmark` compares Busy retry CPU with the by-value path.

Configure at least three protected Control slots, five total permits, and a Control pool covering both the complete election pack and the largest no-op RPC append. The constructor validates these requirements before IO. Idle leader ticks need no campaign pack. After `foreground_burst` foreground operations, ordinary admission pauses until `run_one()` executes reclamation of at most `scan_entries` directory entries. One owed, fully admitted Tick may precede that slice while leaving maintenance due, so sustained reads cannot starve heartbeats or election timers. Pressure proceeds to maintenance, which can return capacity. This bounds foreground work by the configured burst plus one timer opportunity. Callers must pump the worker for progress; filesystem-call latency remains unbounded by this scheduler.

If occupied output or pending persistence prevents that Tick, GC retains timer debt. Ordinary admission cannot take the next idle opening until the private timer path services it. Timer-first and GC-first orderings both preserve the next ordinary foreground budget; maintenance-owned ticks do not consume that budget. Reliable completions, output draining, application failures and bounded reclamation remain available while this admission gate is active.

The isolated worker tests cover actual POSIX persistence/recovery, interrupted initialization, all-or-nothing partial pack failure, busy application completion, multiple actions in one output batch, failure while output is occupied, leader ticks under Control pressure and continuous foreground reads. Detent's authority runs on this Worker; production capacity qualification remains pending.

### Completion-driven append ownership

Construct Worker with `std::shared_ptr<journal::IO>` and set `WorkerLimits::async_appends` to opt into completion-driven initialization and ordinary Raft journal appends. Borrowed backend construction rejects that flag before IO. `run_one()` returns `IOPending` while an operation is outstanding; `take_io_operation()` hands out that operation once. Submit it to an owned completion driver, retain it through every original primitive, and apply completions on the same protocol executor. After the final barrier, a subsequent Worker turn delivers the matching persistence completion. While pending, Worker turns advance Core's safety clock without inventing a persistence acknowledgement.

`storage_operation_failed(operation, reason)` accepts only the current operation capability. It fences Core and storage but does not reap or cancel an accepted primitive; the driver must retain and reap it. Failed or retired operation capabilities cannot release another append. Initialization gates `ready()` until durable settlement. The focused `cluster_worker_test --completion-regressions` suite exercises held completions, exactly-once dispatch, stale failure reports, failure after submission, Worker/backend facade retirement, and a complete election/proposal append chain.

The append flag covers initialization and Raft appends. `WorkerLimits::async_storage` additionally dispatches checkpoint artifact creation, bounded writes, sealing, metadata opening, validation and source reads, publication and reclamation through the same owned completion interface; it also enables asynchronous appends. Reclamation handles one directory entry per maintenance quantum. The default profile remains synchronous. Bootstrap creation, recovery and initial inventory still run synchronously before live service admission; the production service has not yet activated the full profile. Consensus bundle encoding and suffix capture still require CPU work on the owner executor.

### Worker-owned checkpoint lifecycle

Call `reserve_checkpoint(application_cap)` before capturing immutable application state. It reserves the complete application artifact, maximum consensus bundle and publication footprint as one replacement. The returned `CheckpointId` belongs to this worker session; stale or foreign IDs cannot attach state or cancel another replacement. Attach `CaptureMetadata{request, through, term}` exactly once, with the image pinned at committed, applied index `through`.

Stream that image with `offer_application_chunk(id, bytes, final)`. Each chunk is at most 64 KiB and copied into one fixed mailbox. `Busy` leaves the offered bytes unaccepted; retry them after pumping `run_one()`. Aggregate bytes cannot exceed the reserved cap. An empty final chunk is valid, and accepting the final marker immediately closes the stream. The caller retains the immutable capture until staging finishes or cancellation succeeds.

Preparation alternates with bounded reclamation and preserves owed timer service, including while external output is occupied. Application sealing records cutover debt: ordinary admission pauses until the existing persistence chain, output and application completion drain, then Core captures the checkpoint. GC cannot erase that debt. Core stays busy while the worker encodes the bounded suffix, writes the bundle in 64 KiB slices and publishes both artifacts at its exact journal sequence. A fresh Tick precedes delivery of the matching durable completion. Reliable `Applied` remains available throughout.

`cancel_checkpoint()` returns `Canceled` after completed disposal, `Pending` while a matching accepted storage quantum must settle, `TooLate` after Core checkpoint persistence, or `Stale` for another session or completed operation. Repeated pending cancellation is idempotent. `checkpoint_active(id)` remains true through pending disposal. Incoming snapshot cancellation follows the same rule: its first reason is retained, validation and installation are blocked, and one correlated terminal result follows original IO reaping and accounting settlement. Failure still fences rather than concealing an uncertain outcome. The channel retains a canceling receiver through result delivery or disconnected cleanup and consumes already-sent valid data without granting further credit. Known cancellation retains the charges for written artifacts until durable reclamation removes them. Uncertain preparation or publication fences Core and Store. Staging seals artifacts without a separate full readback; reopening verifies every referenced payload and semantic bundle before publishing recovered state. A crash after publication but before Core completion restores the checkpoint and committed suffix. Tests also exercise the first durable proposal and application after publication.

Create a journal explicitly, append `encode_initialization(configuration)`, and construct the empty core only after that append succeeds. Reopen by passing each verified journal batch to `Recovery::replay`, then call `finish(frontier.sequence)` only after `Journal::recover` returns successfully. Semantic replay checks configuration identity, contiguous indexes, monotonic terms and commitment, ballot lifetime, log bounds, and committed-prefix protection. Same-index/same-term entries with different content fail closed. Journal checksums alone do not establish these invariants.

The restarted application rebuilds state only through the durable commit index. External effects need their own idempotency. Do not erase a voter's state and rejoin under its old identity: its forgotten ballot invalidates crash-recovery assumptions. Node replacement and restore epochs require explicit provisioning.

### Terminal retirement

`Worker::close()` permanently closes strict consensus admission. Subsequent `run_one()` calls reap only storage operations that were already created, including operations not yet dispatched through `take_io_operation()`. Callers must retain Worker, IO and the completion driver until original operations settle and `drained()` becomes true. Closing does not fabricate persistence or application acknowledgments. Successful accepted publication may leave snapshot activation for recovery; unpublished replacements settle their known staging debt. Closing suppresses ordinary timers and new persistence, checkpoint and source work.

After accepted originals settle, Worker incrementally releases retained actions and calls Core's terminal retirement API. `Core::begin_retirement()` invalidates checkpoint capabilities and detaches live log roots; `retirement_step(budget)` retires pending actions and physical log entries. The byte budget must fit one maximum legal command, while the unit budget separately bounds empty entries and actions. `retirement_drained()` includes pending protocol containers and retired payloads. Existing synchronous destructors remain fallback behavior for callers that do not use this additive lifecycle; legacy Raft and storage formats are unchanged. Application and transport owners must separately retire their roots and original callbacks before destruction.

Source verification completion always observes the authentic typed IO result before resuming its consumer. A peer failure or terminal close suppresses obsolete source continuation, so a disconnected verifier can be released without dereferencing it when the held original settles. Terminal snapshot validation and activation callbacks return `NotReady`; they neither advance the application nor fence successfully published storage.

### Many groups on one core

`Worker(io, TurnDriver&, group, ...)` opens a group on journal's shared turn log (D11): the core's groups stage their batches and checkpoint records there, and one data barrier per turn persists all of them. A group checkpoints and recovers from its latest checkpoint record, and a per-group journal migrates into the turn log at every crash point (R20). `test/shared_worker_test.cc` and `test/shared_cluster_test.cc` run single- and multi-voter groups through it.

## Local immutable checkpoints

The host pins an immutable application capture at committed, applied index `A`, prepares its artifact on the storage executor, and optionally verifies it before requesting cutover. `LocalCheckpoint` carries that host-owned capture token, `A`, its term, and cluster/configuration identities. The core admits a started, idle request only when its included boundary matches the retained committed log. Followers can checkpoint too. Serialization must use the pinned image at `A`, not mutable application state that has advanced since capture.

`PersistCheckpoint` reserves the same exclusive persistence slot as ordinary storage batches. It captures current durable HardState and every entry after `A`, including uncommitted entries, with the persisted application cursor set to `A`. The ordered storage worker records its exact current journal sequence `S`, encodes a `CheckpointBundle` binding `S` and the application descriptor, prepares the bundle, then publishes both through the journal. `A` is a log/application index; `S` is a storage-operation sequence.

Matching `Persisted` alone compacts the live prefix through `A`. Newer live application progress, outstanding delivered ranges, pending reads, and genuine peer matches survive. Reliable `Applied` completions remain accepted during cutover. Any preparation or readback failure that fences the journal requires trusted local `StorageFault`, which fences the core even without a pending batch. Correlated `Failed` remains appropriate for the current persistence operation.

Recover with the journal's restore callback: require the declared application dependency, decode the bundle against that exact descriptor and `frontier.base_sequence`, call `Recovery::restore`, then replay suffix batches. Call `finish` only after the entire journal recovery succeeds. Recover the application image at `A` and apply committed entries above it again. Never import these local hard-state/configuration bundles as network snapshots; portable installation is a separate protocol.

A compacted follower reports its boundary index and term. A correlated matching hint can choose the next prefix probe but never counts as an acknowledgement. A leader needing unavailable history emits a correlated `SnapshotNeeded` for that peer. The sender flight protocol below bounds waiting and retries; requesting or transmitting an image does not prove successful catch-up. Legacy wire shapes remain unchanged.

The full retained suffix is copied and encoded after freeze. Payload and entry limits bound this work, but production timing must budget its measured pause. Worker-owned Store admission reserves replacement capacity before capture and charges obsolete generations until bounded, durable reclamation removes them. Backend mutation outside the exclusive Store contract invalidates these accounting guarantees.

## Snapshots

### The portable descriptor

`snapshot.h` defines the `SnapshotDescriptor`: cluster/configuration identities, application-format version, included index/term, exact payload length and CRC32C, and, under magic `RFTSNP02`, the membership in force at the boundary. `RFTSNP01`, the fixed 72-byte form without a membership, is still written for fixed-voter groups and read. All integers use fixed little-endian encoding. `SnapshotPolicy` supplies the expected identities, supported format and maximum image length; decoding allocates no application payload. Empty images require the CRC32C of empty input. Format zero is allowed when explicitly supported. Included indexes exclude zero and the maximum integer; terms exclude zero and allow the maximum integer.

`encode_snapshot()` and `decode_snapshot()` validate exact framing and policy. This content excludes sender-local node identity, ballot, commit cursor, journal sequence/generation, artifact names and retained suffix. A receiver constructs its own local checkpoint bundle. CRC32C detects accidental corruption; it provides neither authentication nor a collision-resistant snapshot identity. The future authenticated transfer envelope must bind all descriptor fields and payload bytes. This codec does not install state, reserve disk space, authorize peers, handle leader terms or acknowledge application activation.

### Receiver installation in the core

The transport-free Core accepts trusted `InstallPrepared` after the adapter has completely staged and semantically validated an immutable application image. The event carries a prepared-image token, request correlation, authenticated peer assertion, leader term and included boundary. Core revalidates fixed identities, voter membership, tokens and index/term bounds at cutover. These events and their activation completions reject through ordinary Worker admission; owned transfer and activation use the separate Worker APIs described below.

An image must advance beyond the receiver's durable commit cursor. A caught-up image produces `InstallRejected{..., CaughtUp}` without application rollback. If its valid leader term is higher, that receiver-owned term transition and ballot reset become durable before rejection is exposed. Same-term installation preserves the receiver ballot. A matching local index/term boundary retains the receiver's suffix; an incompatible boundary discards it. `PersistInstall` contains the complete receiver-local configuration, derived hard state, new commit/base boundary and recovered application cursor, never sender-local persistence metadata.

Matching `Persisted` adopts the durable boundary and emits `ActivateInstall`, while Core remains busy. This branch performs no ordinary delivery, quorum-read completion or campaigning. Previously delivered `Committed` work may finish through reliable `Applied` in either phase. Matching `InstallActivated` requires that old range to have drained, advances the live application cursor to the installed boundary, resets the follower election deadline and emits `InstallCompleted`. Premature activation or a matching activation failure fences. Stale tokens cannot release another operation; subsequent campaigning requires a separately admitted Tick.

This increment specifies deterministic receiver transitions. Worker reception and installation are described below; transport authentication and correlated sender acknowledgements remain separate gates. Core's authenticated peer field is a trusted adapter assertion, and CRC32C provides no authentication.

### Validating an incoming image

Worker enables reception only when its optional final constructor argument supplies `SnapshotLimits{application_format, application_bytes}`. Existing callers keep reception disabled. `reserve_snapshot()` checks descriptor policy and sender context before reserving the entire image and maximum receiver-local bundle. `SnapshotContext::authenticated_peer` is a trusted adapter assertion; the adapter must authenticate the transport and bind the descriptor and bytes to that peer. Only configured, nonlocal voters are admitted. Malformed contexts throw without reserving storage or producing a terminal result; capacity or an occupied replacement slot returns no operation.

Local captures and incoming images share one replacement reservation and one 64 KiB mailbox. `offer_snapshot_chunk()` requires exact sequential offsets, bounds each chunk and accepts a final marker only at the declared length. A Busy response consumes no bytes. The final marker closes input immediately. Sealing compares the received length and checksum with the descriptor before incremental rereading begins. A mismatch is a healthy `InvalidImage` result; local read failures or reread checksum corruption fence the entire Worker.

`validation_chunk()` exposes one borrowed data view at a time. Its bytes and token remain stable across Worker turns and ordinary traffic until matching `consume_validation()`, cancellation, fencing or Worker destruction. The application must copy or finish parsing before consuming it and must not retain the span afterward. The application owns parser allocation limits and semantic checks. Every view has a distinct token; stale, duplicate, foreign-session and wrong-operation acknowledgements cannot release the mailbox. Verified EOF appears only after the complete reread checksum succeeds. `validation_succeeded()` accepts only the consumed verified-EOF token and leaves the candidate validated but unpublished.

`cancel_snapshot()` and `reject_snapshot_validation()` close validation handles, release reservation ownership and leave actual staged bytes charged until bounded reclamation completes. A separate `take_snapshot_result()` slot preserves correlation and blocks another local or incoming replacement until drained. It does not block ordinary protocol traffic. Reception, held views and semantic waiting leave protocol timers and bounded reclamation available. Worker operations, callbacks, spans and storage objects remain confined to the owning executor.

Staging and validation alone do not change Core's incoming boundary, publish a receiver checkpoint, activate application state or acknowledge installation to a sender. Installation requires the separate explicit request below. Restart recovers the selected old frontier and reclaims abandoned unpublished images rather than activating them.

### Installing and activating

`request_install(id)` requires a validated candidate and reserves one conservative Control append before accepting cutover. Pressure leaves the candidate validated and ordinary traffic available. The permit remains separate from existing protocol continuation packs. Accepted requests latch cutover debt, drain existing persistence and ordinary output, then let Core revalidate the stored sender context and derive receiver-local state. Matching request retries reserve nothing further. Pre-Core cancellation refunds the dedicated permit; cancellation becomes TooLate after either installation publication work or higher-term rejection persistence begins.

An immediate Core rejection cancels staged storage and produces a correlated `Rejected` result. Higher-term caught-up rejection first completes its receiver-owned HardState write. An advancing image captures the exact current receiver sequence and reuses the local checkpoint bundle pipeline, including bounded chunk writes and Store publication. Incoming and local operations move their captured state into the same encoder without retaining an additional suffix copy. Successful Store publication preserves the incoming operation until application activation completes.

`snapshot_activation()` repeatedly exposes the same session-bound request after durable publication, once previously delivered application work and retained Applied events have drained. It does not consume ordinary output capacity. The application retains its validated candidate, switches application state atomically, then reports the exact request with `snapshot_activated(id, token)`. Worker retains that completion independently of ordinary output, refreshes time while Core remains busy, and delivers it when output capacity allows. Only matching Core completion produces `Installed`, releases cutover and permits ordinary admission again. A matching `snapshot_activation_failed(id, token)` fences immediately; published generations never roll back. Previously delivered application failure remains reliable throughout installation.

Core rejection and installation results carry an optional durable receiver term. Generic preparation cancellations do not claim a durable term. Terminal results remain separate from ordinary output and block new replacement operations until taken. Maintenance remains bounded and eligible during output or activation waits. Applications must recover their selected persisted image independently of transient transfer context; reopening after publication restores that image even when live activation or its acknowledgment never completed. This API does not authenticate a network transfer, correlate sender replication progress or establish production pause budgets.

### Sender flights

Core retains at most one snapshot flight per configured remote voter. `SnapshotNeeded` carries the peer, durable leader term, checked transfer token and exact included boundary. A trusted `SnapshotSourceReady` must match all fields and the current compacted boundary before Core emits `SnapshotTransmit`. Duplicate or obsolete callbacks do not restart the timeout. `SnapshotTransferFailed` releases the flight; a failure during transmission first retries an ordinary prefix probe after backoff. A failure while awaiting a source requests a fresh source after backoff.

`Timing::snapshot_timeout` defaults to 60,000 in the same monotonic units as the other timing fields. Expiration releases the flight and schedules a fresh ordinary prefix probe. Backoff prevents repeated events at a fixed clock, including the maximum clock value, from continuously allocating transfers. A changed boundary invalidates an awaiting source. While transmitting, Core rejects local compaction beyond the selected boundary so the subsequent log suffix remains available.

During either phase, independently paced empty Append messages keep the receiver in contact with its leader. Their genesis boundary, empty payload and zero commit/read probe convey no replication, commitment or read-quorum evidence. Replies do not erase the pacing timestamp. Ordinary healthy peers remain able to replicate while an image is pending.

Only an exact active transmitting flight accepts `SnapshotResponse::Installed`. It advances that peer through the included boundary, then requires ordinary Append evidence for subsequent progress and reads. `CaughtUp` requests a fresh ordinary prefix probe without advancing match; `Rejected` releases the flight without advancing match. Malformed responses and nonmembers are rejected before observing their term. A valid higher receiver term follows ordinary durable step-down: term persistence precedes visible role and release actions. Hosts must release all source resources on `Fenced`, including when a failed term write prevents individual release actions from becoming visible.

This is an isolated Core protocol, not a streaming transport. Worker ordinary admission rejects the trusted source callbacks; the owned source and transfer adapter below deliver them internally. Transport must authenticate peers and bind descriptor, payload, process session and transfer identity. Receiver preparation failures without a usable durable term map to the trusted failure callback rather than a fabricated Raft response. Legacy wire shapes remain unchanged.

Sender tests cover stale and wrong-peer correlation, installed/caught-up/rejected responses, timeout and lost acknowledgment probes, failed transmission probes, fixed-clock heartbeat pacing, follower contact during long transfers, healthy-peer progress, read barriers, compaction retention, durable higher-term failure, restart epoch separation and all 30 remote voters at the configured maximum. The existing checked token-exhaustion guard is source-reviewed; the sender tests do not dynamically drive the private counter to exhaustion.

### Application format provenance

Version 2 local checkpoint bundles persist an explicit application-format value, including format zero. Version 1 bundles remain readable and represent an unknown format. The encoder writes version 1 when no format is supplied, preserving existing callers, and writes version 2 when `CheckpointBundle::application_format` or the optional encoder argument is supplied. Decoding retains this provenance independently of current runtime configuration. An unknown legacy format cannot safely be inferred from current snapshot policy; the source adapter must require a newly captured, explicitly formatted checkpoint before exporting it.

### Sources and the snapshot channel

Worker discovers awaiting source flights fairly across its peers. A completed publication cache binds Core's included boundary, selected generation and persisted application format. Local checkpoint completion updates it after matching Core persistence; incoming installation updates it only after application activation completes. Recovery establishes it after successful journal/application restoration. Physical manifest publication alone cannot authorize a source. Known format-policy mismatches fail recovery before application restoration, and unknown v1 formats remain non-exportable.

One outbound source is independent of the incoming/local replacement slot. It opens the selected dependency, verifies its entire checksum incrementally, promotes the same file/pin, then revalidates generation, boundary and exact flight before delivering trusted source readiness. Only Core's matching transmit phase exposes `snapshot_source()`. Streaming rereads at most 64 KiB per source turn into one mailbox; `source_chunk()` remains stable until the matching `consume_source(id, token)`. An empty final view follows the complete image. Sending retains the source pin until Core releases the flight. `source_failed(id)` and the trusted peer-close notification retain failure callbacks across ordinary busy/output conditions. Tentative leadership loss invalidates the source before a durable public role action. IO/checksum failures fence storage.

Maintenance rotates through checkpoint preparation, source work and reclamation, skipping blocked work while preserving timer debt. Held inbound and outbound views leave ordinary reads, healthy-voter proposals, timer contact and reclamation available. Borrowed views expire on consumption, cancellation, fencing or owner destruction; all APIs remain on one owning executor.

`snapshot_transfer.h` provides a versioned 128-byte envelope with exact framing, configuration identities, source/destination nodes, both connection identities and the complete Core key. Begin carries the portable descriptor. Chunk carries a token, offset, bounded payload and final flag. Accepted, Credit, Result and Cancel use bounded control bodies. Reserved fields and exact lengths are validated before dispatch. This narrow opaque bulk format does not choose the future authority/public transaction encoding.

`SnapshotChannel` connects Workers over a caller-supplied `TrustedSession`: an assertion from an authenticated, reliable, ordered transport, with fresh identities on reconnect. Frame fields and CRC do not authenticate anyone. Register only configured remote voters. `receive()` accepts fragmented input, dispatches at most one complete frame per call and reports consumed bytes, including bytes retained under Busy; retry only the unconsumed remainder. `run_one()` retries retained frames and installation admission. The host separately runs Worker, drains ordinary actions, supplies application semantic validation and performs the exact application activation callback.

`outbound()` retains frame bytes across partial writes. Consume its exact token and byte count only after transport accepts them. A partially written frame stays selected until completion or its session closes, including when another peer has pending output. The host must enforce a bounded write-stall deadline and close stalled sessions. Each configured peer has one bounded decoder and a separate small control slot; there is one outbound data frame at a time, and up to `SnapshotLimits::window` chunks in flight awaiting credit (one by default). Credits are cumulative: a Credit covers every chunk up to the one it names, and an unsent Credit is replaced by the next chunk's, so the receiver keeps pace with a window. With a window of 1 the frames are those of stop-and-wait, so windowed and stop-and-wait nodes interoperate. Credit means acceptance into the receiver-owned mailbox, not durable storage. Busy creates no credit. Sequential offsets and exact tokens prevent replayed data from being reapplied. Matching duplicate Accepted replies are idempotent. Duplicate Begin reuses an active operation or the channel's single cached terminal outcome; conflicting descriptors close that session without fencing healthy local storage.

Only Worker’s final installation result can generate an Installed frame. The sender retains a received result under ordinary Busy/Pressure and submits it through funded Core admission. Higher durable terms still reach Core for obsolete keys. Outcomes without a usable nonzero durable term become correlated source failures, including Busy rejection before Core Start. Connection loss cancels staging when legal. TooLate preserves the application candidate and completes local publication/activation independently; the disconnected terminal result is drained locally. Sender retry uses Core's bounded deadline and ordinary prefix probe. There is no chunk retransmission, reconnect resume, socket implementation or TLS in this adapter.

Payload-buffer capacity is fixed by voter count rather than image length: one 65,688-byte decoder per configured remote voter, at most one 65,688-byte data frame per adapter, control frames of at most 200 bytes per peer, a bounded terminal cache and one 64 KiB Worker source mailbox. An independent incoming/local operation adds its existing 64 KiB mailbox. Chunk encoding temporarily allocates at most one additional 64 KiB body. `retained_bytes()` counts occupied frame bytes; `buffer_capacity()` includes idle decoder arrays and output-string capacity; Worker exposes its mailbox capacity separately. These counts exclude retained Raft/application state, checkpoint encoding, allocator overhead and IO-handle internals. Worker must outlive its channel. Before destroying a channel, close each registered session and continue running the channel, Worker and required application callbacks until `drained()` reports that its incoming operation and terminal ownership are gone. TooLate installation must finish or fence through the existing application failure API. Observing terminal Worker fencing closes sessions and clears channel-owned metadata, so teardown drains without emitting a result. Dropping a channel early does not cancel irreversible Worker state.

The tests connect three actual Workers on independent POSIX stores, retain a healthy voting peer during transfer and apply the ordinary suffix after receiver activation. Cases include fragmented input, partial output, empty/multiple-chunk images, credit backpressure, malformed/session-mismatched frames, duplicate Begin, pre-publication disconnect/reconnect, post-publication disconnect, lost final result and fresh prefix recovery. Source tests cover foreign/stale callbacks, metadata/read/CRC faults, simultaneous held inbound/outbound views and healthy commitment. Existing reopen schedules cover receiver publication and activation windows; these are normal-close tests, not abrupt process-kill tests. Older strict readers reject new v2 bundles; legacy Raft APIs and wire formats remain unchanged.

### Application runtime observations

`Worker::retained_log_bytes()` reports the sum of Core's retained command payload lengths, excluding journal and entry framing, for proactive application checkpoint thresholds. `Worker::base_boundary()` reports the recovered or installed index and term so application recovery can verify its exact image boundary. `Worker::snapshot_context(id)` returns the immutable incoming descriptor only for a matching live capability; foreign, canceled and fenced capabilities expose no metadata. These read-only observations perform no IO and change neither legacy Raft APIs nor wire formats.

## Peer transport

The optional target `consensus::transport` (`include/consensus/transport/`, on with
`CONSENSUS_TRANSPORT`, always on when this repository builds itself) carries the core's
messages between voters: TLS 1.2 or later over TCP through `reactor::tls`, a control lane
and a bulk lane per peer, each voter's leaf certificate pinned by SHA-256, the `DTHL0001`
hello (cluster, group, epoch, connection identity, and a profile fingerprint both ends
must share), the `DTC2` message codec, bounded byte credit in both directions, snapshot
writes, and RPC timing. It needs Asio (through `reactor`) and OpenSSL 3.

`Peers<Host>` runs it on one executor for any host with `worker()` and `channel()` (the
`PeerHost` concept), configured by a `PeerConfiguration`: the voters, the consensus
limits, the group's identities, and the fingerprint from `profile_fingerprint`, which
hashes the application's own profile bytes with the membership and limits.

## Bounds

Limits cap voters, entry counts, individual command payloads, aggregate retained payloads, RPC entry counts and payloads, uncommitted entry count, and pending reads. `log_bytes` and `rpc_bytes` are payload budgets; entry counts separately bound object/framing overhead. The storage decoder caps payload and framing before allocation. A future wire decoder must independently cap the complete encoded and decompressed message before building these typed events, and the transport must bound its own queues.

Admission stops before the retained-log entry limit, reserving control entries for elections. Exhausting that finite reserve prevents publishing another leader without its no-op. Term exhaustion never wraps. Checkpoint recovery requires an exact included index/term and an application cursor at that included index. Checkpoints reclaim retained history. Production capacity and fleet qualification remain later gates.

## Testing

`test/simulation_test.cc` checks randomized groups against election safety, leader completeness, log matching, one applied prefix, one grant per voter per term, and durability when sent, under loss, reordering, duplication, partitions, skewed clocks, and crashes; `tools/mutants.sh` seeds known faults and requires the harness to catch each. `test/liveness_test.cc` measures recovery and hand-off under failures, `test/membership_test.cc` and `test/pipeline_test.cc` cover membership changes and pipelining, and `test/compat_test.cc` with `tools/compat.sh` checks that stores written by `b7073bb` recover and that `b7073bb` refuses the new formats. `fuzz/` fuzzes every decoder. These are schedule coverage, not exhaustive model checking. [Raft's paper](https://raft.github.io/raft.pdf) is the algorithm reference; [journal's ARCHITECTURE.md](https://github.com/Kronuz/journal/blob/main/ARCHITECTURE.md) records the filesystem and hardware assumptions.
